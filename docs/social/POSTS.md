𝗜 𝘀𝗽𝗹𝗶𝘁 𝗺𝘆 𝗺𝗮𝗻𝗮𝗴𝗲𝗺𝗲𝗻𝘁 𝗮𝘂𝘁𝗼𝗺𝗮𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻 𝗶𝗻𝘁𝗼 𝘁𝗵𝗿𝗲𝗲 𝗿𝗲𝗽𝗼𝘀𝗶𝘁𝗼𝗿𝗶𝗲𝘀, 𝗮𝗻𝗱 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘀𝗽𝗹𝗶𝘁 𝗶𝘀 𝘁𝗵𝗲 𝘄𝗵𝗼𝗹𝗲 𝗱𝗲𝘀𝗶𝗴𝗻.

𝟭. 𝗪𝗿𝗶𝘁𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗶𝘀 𝗼𝗽𝗲𝗻 𝘀𝗼𝘂𝗿𝗰𝗲. Leadership-Writing-Skills: talent cards, work summaries, promo docs, status updates, plus a critic that grades every draft. Pure functions. They never fetch and never publish. A 20-line org profile swaps in your company's words for principles, ratings and levels.

𝟮. 𝗣𝗹𝘂𝗺𝗯𝗶𝗻𝗴 𝗶𝘀 𝗼𝗽𝗲𝗻 𝘀𝗼𝘂𝗿𝗰𝗲. manager-ops-kernel: one JSON file per unit of automation, a typed graph, six invariants a checker refuses to break, and a runner for Claude Code, Codex, Kiro CLI and Kiro Crew. About ten lines per harness.

𝟯. 𝗡𝗼𝘂𝗻𝘀 𝘀𝘁𝗮𝘆 𝗽𝗿𝗶𝘃𝗮𝘁𝗲. Tool names, document ids, channels, rubrics, people. Yours, in your own repo.

Every cell is the same seven-stage loop: 𝘁𝗿𝗶𝗴𝗴𝗲𝗿 → 𝗴𝗮𝘁𝗵𝗲𝗿 → 𝗱𝗶𝗳𝗳 → 𝘄𝗿𝗶𝘁𝗲 → 𝗴𝗮𝘁𝗲 → 𝗹𝗮𝗻𝗱 → 𝗿𝗲𝗰𝗼𝗿𝗱.

Three rules I will not bend:
▸ Writers never fetch. Gatherers never write.
▸ Nothing lands ungated. Two to five critic passes, no invented facts.
▸ Record it, or it did not happen. Each level's ledger is the next level's source.

And one cut: people work (1:1s, hiring, talent) runs in its own lane with its own memory. Only drafts and sanitised ledger lines leave it. The checker rejects a ledger field named rating, candidate or promo.

Dry-ran the same cell on all four harnesses this week. Four gated drafts, zero em-dashes, every number traceable to the fixture.
