# Template: Work Summary

Title: `# {NAME} - Work Summary`

No rating, tier, or paraphrase of one appears anywhere in this document.

## 1. Summary table (at the very top, before any detail section)

A single table that lets a reader grasp the body of work at a glance:

| Category | Project Title | Project Summary | Impact | Collaborators |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Impact] | <project> | <one line> | <quantified outcome> | <names/teams> |

- **Category** is one of: `[Ambiguity / Technical Complexity]`, `[Scope and Influence]`,
  `[Impact]`, `[Operational Excellence]`, `Known Gaps and Honest Assessment`.
- Sort rows by the candidate's IMPACT, highest first.
- The detail sections below MUST appear in the same order as the rows here.

## 2. Detail sections

Group projects under capability section headers matching the categories above, for
example `## [Ambiguity / Technical Complexity]`, `## [Scope and Influence]`,
`## [Impact]`, `## [Operational Excellence]`. Place each project under the capability it
best evidences, and keep the overall order aligned with the summary table (impact-sorted).

For EACH project: a `### <n>. <Project Name>` heading, then these labeled fields IN THIS
ORDER. Every field is a NARRATIVE PARAGRAPH (not bullets), except Artifacts:

- **Summary**: 2-4 sentence narrative of what they did and why it mattered.
- **Timeline**: the months/quarters the work spanned, in prose; include all periods the
  evidence covers.
- **Task**: what they were asked to accomplish.
- **Challenges**: the genuinely hard parts (technical complexity, ambiguity, cross-team
  coordination, risk), written as prose.
- **Action**: the specific actions THIS person took, written as prose. This is the
  substance of the example; attribute precisely (led vs contributed to).
- **Result**: measurable outcomes with real numbers, written as prose. Label any figure
  that is a projection or test result rather than a realized production outcome.
- **Artifacts**: a short bulleted list of real links from the supplied evidence (code
  reviews, design docs, tickets, dashboards, recognition).

Include work from every period the evidence spans, earliest to latest within each section.

## 3. Known Gaps and Honest Assessment

A candid narrative section naming what is unproven, still developing, or unverified. Do
not soften it into vague positivity; this is what keeps the document credible.
